Assignment 2: White Hat vs. Black Hat


White Hat Visualization:

The Influence of Gender on Global Education Trend

Black Hat Visualization:

The Global Connection between Female Adolescent (15-19) Fertility and Life Expectancy


White Hat Write-Up


For our white-hat visualization, we used the dataset “Gender Equality Indicators 1960–2017.” This dataset includes a wide range of measures such as fertility rates, literacy rates, and education metrics to reflect gender equality across countries. Instead of trying to include every variable, we focused specifically on education by looking at enrollment and completion rates across different levels. The main measures we used were primary school enrollment rates, average number of children out of school, and lower, upper, and post-secondary completion rates for both males and females. These measures help show whether youth in different countries are able to access and complete education, and how those outcomes differ by gender.


To present this information clearly, we paid close attention to layout and color choices. Since the dashboard includes six graphs, we organized them in a way that guides the viewer naturally. The layout follows a left-to-right and top-to-bottom structure, so users start in the top left and move across and down. There is also a clear visual separation in the middle to reinforce this flow. All filters are placed in the top left since they control the entire dashboard, making them easy to find and use first. Next to the filters, we included a legend to clarify that one color represents females and another represents males, since all graphs rely on comparing the two groups.


We also grouped the visualizations into two main sections: Access to Education and Completion of Education. Instead of presenting all graphs at once, we added short descriptions for each section to help explain the overall patterns. The structure of the dashboard is intentional. We start with the gender gap index shown on the map, and then move into more detailed charts like enrollment rates, out-of-school averages, and completion rates. This helps break down how the index is formed and what contributes to it. To keep things transparent, we included the formula for the gender gap index at the bottom of the dashboard. Tooltips were also added to every graph to display exact values and years, which helps users interpret smaller visuals more accurately. Lastly, we removed data from 2019 and 2020 due to a noticeable drop in available data, since including those years could lead to misleading conclusions.




Black Hat Write-Up


For the black-hat visualization, we decided to focus on adolescent (ages 15-19) fertility rates and female life expectancy. The goal of the black hat visualization is to subtly portray a relationship between these two unrelated variables and to paint certain countries and regions of the world in a worse light.


The information is visualized using a map and bar chart. Starting with the map, each country is represented with a color and a circle mark of varying sizes. The color represents the adolescent fertility rate, and the size represents life expectancy. Color is represented with a green to red gradient, with red representing higher fertility rate, and green representing lower fertility rate. This scale was manually manipulated to adjust the “center” of the gradient to be lower, allowing for more countries to be marked as red, even if they are technically above the world average. This disproportionately shows entire regions of the world like Africa, South America, and parts of Asia as red. To further amplify the focus on these areas, the size markings with circles are used. Large circles are directly stated as indicating lower life expectancy. While that is technically true, the actual life expectancies were manipulated behind the scenes. They were first subtracted from 100 (100 - life exp.) and then squared, and these values were used for the size of the circle. This made countries with smaller life expectancies appear disproportionately larger compared to those with higher life expectancies. This further brings focus to countries in Africa, Asia, and parts of South America as they have large red circles while other places like China, USA, and Europe all have small, green circles that go unnoticed.


The bar chart further uses black hat techniques to push a narrative. First off, only the countries with the highest and lowest adolescent fertility rates for a given year are chosen. The color scheme is the exact same as that used by the map for consistency. Life expectancy, however, is represented differently compared to the map. While the data was altered in the map to make low life expectancy mean a large mark, in the bar chart, low life expectancy means a skinnier bar, and high life expectancy means a larger bar. This inconsistency subtly pushes a narrative here that the countries with the highest fertility rates have worse life expectancies. The life expectancy is manipulated mathematically similar to that for the map to exaggerate the differences.


Other details of the visualization subtly add to the black hat design. Wording choices like “the global connection”, “concentrations in the global south”, and “relationship between” all contribute to the idea that the two factors are connected. Also, only a single year dropdown is available. This purposefully restricts viewers' ability to see change over time. If the viewer also chooses to do a deep-dive into any specific country, the tool tip only shows the correct values and not the modified values. On the surface, everything appears trustworthy, but the underlying transformations completely alter the message.




Overview


This assignment was evenly split between Tiffany and Ryan. Since there were 2 individuals in this group, and Assignment 2 had 2 main sections, we decided to split it up based on those sections. Tiffany focused on the white-hat visualization, while Ryan focused on the black-hat visualization.


For the white-hat visualization, Tiffany spent approximately 8 hours completing the project. She began the development process by examining each column in the dataset to understand the types of information available for representing global gender equality indicators. Noticing a strong emphasis on education-related metrics, she chose educational progress as the focus of her visualization. The dataset included multiple completion rates, ranging from primary school enrollment to doctoral degree attainment, which offered an opportunity to explore patterns in education across different stages of life.


To analyze these trends, Tiffany created multiple sheets in Tableau, plotting year on the X-axis and completion rate on the Y-axis for each level of education. She then compared male and female completion rates to highlight disparities between genders. However, she found it difficult to clearly interpret the magnitude of these gaps, so she developed a calculation that subtracts the average female completion rate from the average male completion rate. This computed value was then visualized on a world map, allowing her to identify countries with larger gender-based disparities in educational attainment. The hardest aspect for her was trying to create a layout that told a story with so many graphs to choose from. Inevitably, she removed the Bachelor’s and Doctorate’s graph as it didn’t provide enough information compared to the other visualizations. It was also difficult to put all these visualizations onto one dashboard without the dashboard looking cluttered.


For the black-hat visualization, it also took Ryan around 8 hours to complete. To start development, Ryan looked at what columns had the most data, and he saw that adolescent fertility rate and female life expectancy both had plenty of data. These two indicators in particular caught his eye since it can be easy to manipulate the narrative to make it look like these factors are connected. For the black-hat aspect, Ryan also decided that doing a map visualization would be useful since certain regions could be highlighted and catch the user's eye. After creating the map, Ryan also realized that another way to strengthen the narrative would be to have a bar chart of the highest and lowest fertility rate countries for a given year. He created these visualizations in Tableau since it has many of those features built in and it provides an easy way to manipulate scales, data, and titles. Creating the dashboard took the longest time due to formatting, making things like the year dropdown work for both visualizations, and creating subtitles.
